title: Building in public, failing in private

Sharing work in public is useful. Sharing every half-formed thought is noise. I spent a year optimizing for visibility when I should have optimized for depth.

## The performance trap

A launch thread feels like progress. A screenshot with green CI badges feels like proof. Neither replaces the slow work of making something reliable enough that you would recommend it to a friend.

Building in public works when the artifact is real: a shipped feature, a measured improvement, a lesson earned from production.

## What stays private

Early architecture debates. Half-broken experiments. The third rewrite that taught you what not to do.

That work is not shameful—it is unfinished. Privacy gives you room to be wrong without performing confidence.

## A balance that helps

I share outcomes and principles. I keep drafts and dead ends in a notes folder until they earn a sentence someone else can use.

The goal is not secrecy. The goal is signal: when I publish, it should be because something changed in how I think or build—not because the calendar said Tuesday.

That discipline makes the public writing smaller. It also makes it truer.
