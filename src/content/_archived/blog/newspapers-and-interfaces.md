Print designers worked under brutal constraints: fixed columns, ink limits, and readers who would abandon the page in seconds. The solutions they invented—hierarchy, rhythm, and generous whitespace—map directly to screens.

## Hierarchy is a contract

A headline promises importance. A subhead promises scope. Body copy promises detail. When digital interfaces blur those roles—everything bold, everything animated—the reader has no map.

Newspapers teach you to earn attention in order: scan, orient, then read.

## Whitespace is not waste

Margins are not empty space. They are breathing room for comprehension. Cramped dashboards and hero sections that fill every pixel feel productive in a mockup and exhausting in use.

## Consistency beats novelty

The same byline placement on every page. The same caption style on every photo. Readers learn the system once and spend their energy on content.

Interfaces that reinvent navigation on every screen ask users to relearn the product instead of using it.

## What I take into code

When I design a portfolio or a product surface, I ask editorial questions:

- What is the one thing someone should see first?
- What can be removed without losing meaning?
- Does this layout still work if the content is twice as long?

Those questions predate React. They will outlive it. That is why they are worth keeping.
